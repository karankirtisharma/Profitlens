## 1. Decision in brief

Recommend a small B2B service-business investigation companion, initially for monthly service billing and explicitly allocated delivery costs. Preserve the current application. Complete supported CSV imports, monthly customer contribution, three discrepancy rules and authorized source-backed AI explanations. Retain explicit rejection of unsupported fields and missing-data warnings. General organization configuration, settlement/allocation engines and industry features are deferred. Accounting products already provide profitability and reconciliation; a dashboard or RAG component alone is not differentiation.

**Product hypothesis, not established market fact:** a finance reviewer using separate exports and contract PDFs could resolve a customer/month exception more reliably when the calculation, validation decisions, reviewed applicable contract terms, incomplete-data warnings and original sources are presented together. Test against a prepared spreadsheet and source-folder workflow, then an available accounting-product workflow. Do not assert that competitors cannot provide the same outcome.

## 4. Case-study lessons and limits

- **Contract leakage/fragmentation — M14:** disconnected agreements can obstruct obligation review. Minor-project recommendation: show the reviewed applicable contract page beside each pricing finding; automated amendment/version handling is future work. Do not copy an enterprise integration programme into the minor project.
- **Invoice reconciliation — M15:** consolidated bills can need several trip references. Future-work recommendation: explicit allocation/link entities with failure records; no trip or payment-allocation module is required now. The example supports the existence of heterogeneity, not a universal error rate.
- **Duplicate payment — M16:** duplicate detection sits within a wider AP workflow. Recommendation: distinguish repeated file bytes, repeated transaction identity and merely similar candidates. No automatic deletion based on similarity.
- **Customer profitability/cost-to-serve — M04, M08, M17:** profitability depends on how time and shared costs are attributed. Recommendation: disclose allocation rules and coverage. No independently validated small-service-business cost-to-serve case study was established in this review; this is a research gap.

## 5. Minor-project scope and optional future product validation

Target: small B2B service organizations with supported invoice/cost exports, fixed monthly contract terms where applicable, and a reviewer who can confirm cost coverage. Start with source import and explanation of recorded contribution; avoid statutory profit, tax filing, bank execution and inventory valuation.

The interview/trial numbers below are **optional future product-research suggestions, not college acceptance gates**. The minor project is evaluated through the frozen benchmark, core regression tests and a faculty demonstration. No recruitment programme is required.

| Hypothesis | Proposed validation | Decision criterion (target, not result) |
|---|---|---|
| H1: mapping and source hunting are material pain | Interview 5–8 consenting owners/bookkeepers; observe 3 anonymized month-end workflows | At least 3 independently describe the target task and provide permission-safe examples; otherwise revise scope |
| H2: reviewable evidence improves investigation | 6–10 users, counterbalanced spreadsheet vs ProfitLens tasks, identical evidence | Report correctness, completion time and source-location success; proceed only with no loss in correctness and a useful observed benefit |
| H3: data sufficient for meaningful contribution | Inventory invoice, cost and allocation availability before onboarding | No promise of complete margin without a defined cost boundary and evidence |
| H4: explicit optional evidence avoids forced relationships | Test contract-free and contract-using organizations | Both complete valid imports without invented fields or relationships |

No interviews, customer adoption, causal savings or product superiority were measured in this phase. Stop expansion if users need ledger posting, jurisdictional tax advice or deep ERP replacement rather than investigation.

## 6. What to explain in the viva

ProfitLens does not replace QuickBooks, Xero or an ERP. It demonstrates a bounded investigation flow: a customer margin changes, code computes the values, a rule identifies a reviewable discrepancy, and the user can inspect records and contract evidence behind the explanation. The engineering depth is validation, exact arithmetic, permissions, source retrieval and verification. Wider accounting integrations are future scope.
